# STARGATE — Research Dossier (compiled 2026-10-22)

The U.S. government's 23-year psychic-intelligence program: lineage, people, methods,
landmark sessions, evaluation, documents. Sources: CIA FOIA Reading Room (CREST),
NSA Archive (GWU), SRI final reports, the 1995 AIR evaluation, and program histories.
Every number carries its source; contested claims are flagged, not averaged.

## 1. Program lineage (the codename ladder)

| Years | Codename | Host | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1950s–60s | (watching brief) | CIA | Soviet parapsychology monitored: reportedly 20+ research centers, ~$21M/yr estimated Soviet budget (Mörck, *Star Gate Program Over the Years*). |
| 1972–1976 | **SCANATE** | SRI, Menlo Park | Targ & Puthoff; informational (RV, precognition) and causal (PK) psi. 1973: coordinate RV ("scanning by coordinate") with Swann & Price. |
| 1974 | first operational Soviet target | SRI/CIA | Pat Price, Semipalatinsk (see §4). |
| 1976–1977 | **GONDOLA WISH** | Army ACSI Systems Exploitation Detachment | Dale Graff; evaluates adversary RV applications. |
| 1978–1983 | **GRILL FLAME** | INSCOM Det G, Ft. Meade (bldgs 2560/2561) | Operational unit; first mission Sept 1979 (aircraft crash located within ~15 miles). |
| 1980 | funding cut & reversal | DoD | William Perry terminates R&D funding 5 Mar 1980; reversed 1983. |
| 1983–1985 | **CENTER LANE (ICLP)** | INSCOM | SRI research integrated; 1984 NRC review unfavorable; Jack Anderson reports existence. |
| 1985–1991 | **SUN STREAK** | DIA, DT-S | MOA 18 Sep 1984; Army funding ends late 1985; formal transfer Jan 1986. 1985 McNear CRV manual; 1986 DIA CRV manual. |
| 1991–1995 | **STAR GATE** | DIA → CIA | SAIC replaces SRI; SAP→LIMDIS. Ed May's shop produces ~70% of contractor budget and ~85% of data collection (FAS). |
| 1995 | evaluation & closure | CIA/AIR | June: AIR contracted; 30 June: closure ordered; 29 Sept: report published; declassification follows. |

Money & people: ~$20M total ($19.993M by May's accounting) over 23 years; ~40 personnel
incl. ~23 remote viewers; peak ~7 full-time viewers + analysts; 3 viewers at Ft. Meade
at the end (~$500k/yr final budget, Time 1995).

## 2. The method — CRV stages I–VI (McNear, Feb 1985, CIA-RDP96-00788R001000400001-7)

Coordinate Remote Viewing: "perceiving information concerning a site remote in location
and/or time given only the geographic coordinates." Structure is the discipline; the
enemy is **AOL — Analytical Overlay** (the brain filling in too few data bits with a
plausible picture).

| Stage | Name | What the viewer does |
|---|---|---|
| I | Major Gestalt | Instant kinesthetic "squiggle" = the **ideogram** (A: feeling/motion, B: automatic analytic response). Visuals are labeled AOL on sight. |
| II | Sensory Contact | Non-visual sensations: sounds, smells, tastes, textures, temperatures, energies. |
| III | Dimension, Motion, Mobility | Aperture widens: dimensionals (height/width/distance), aesthetic impact (AI break), trackers (dotted autonomic trace), sketches. |
| IV | General Qualitative Aspects | The operational threshold: matrix columns S-2 · D · AI · EI · T · I · AOL · A/S — activities, objects, and *emotions of people at the site*. |
| V | Specific Analytical Aspects | **Interrogating the signal line** — extracting detail without manufacturing AOL; also used to look *through* an AOL to the data that caused it. |
| VI | 3-D Contact & Modeling | Physical models of the site. |

Session control: minimal monitor input (date/time, coordinates, feedback only). Feedback
vocabulary: Site (done) · C (correct) · PC (probably correct) · N (near) · CFB (can't
feedback). Negative feedback is never given during a session. Summary required after
Stage IV and all operational sessions. Related later methods: ERV (Extended RV) and
written variants; the Gateway/Monroe EBT method is a separate tradition (see Gateway
files, Ch 05).

## 3. The people

- **Hal Puthoff & Russell Targ** — SRI physicists; built the lab evidence and the protocols.
- **Ingo Swann** — artist; co-created CRV with Puthoff (1976–86 development window).
- **Pat Price** — retired police commissioner; the 1974 Semipalatinsk session.
- **Joseph McMoneagle** — "Viewer 001"; the 1979 Typhoon session; later *Mind Trek*.
- **Dale Graff** — Gondola Wish; early Ft. Meade direction (recruited Rosemary Smith).
- **Frederick "Skip" Atwater** — operations officer to 1987; later Monroe Institute president.
- **Maj. Gen. Albert Stubblebine** — INSCOM patron (and the reason the unit existed at all).
- **Tom McNear** — wrote the Feb 1985 CRV training manual.
- **Edwin May** — SAIC era; presided over most late-program data collection.
- **Lyn Buchanan, Paul H. Smith, David Morehouse, Mel Riley** — later viewers/trainers.
- **Jessica Utts & Ray Hyman** — the 1995 external evaluators (statistician vs skeptic).

## 5. The 1995 evaluation and the end

- June 1995: CIA's Office of Research and Development contracts **American Institutes
  for Research** for a congressionally-mandated external review (AIR document, GWU
  NSAEBB438/doc_57).
- The review itself: AIR examined selected SRI/SAIC research and the operational record.
- **Utts**: "Using the standards applied to any other area of science… psychic
  functioning has been well established" — but effect sizes too small and inconsistent
  for intelligence use.
- **Hyman**: "The case for psychic functioning is better than it ever has been" — but
  too weak and inconsistent to count as evidence for psi; data "vague, general, and way
  off target."
- **Both agreed**: no demonstrated operational utility; the program should not continue
  as an intelligence activity.
- Closure was ordered **30 June 1995 — before the report's 29 September publication**;
  a 2023 AFIO thesis calls this a "rush to prejudgement" and notes AIR reviewed ~10
  experiments, not the thousands of operational sessions. Mörck's archive volumes add
  the counterweight: 17 of 19 tasking agencies returned for more RV.
- AIR's bottom line for operations: **no remote-viewing report ever provided actionable
  information for any intelligence operation.**

## 7. Assessment

**Established:** the program existed at scale, was methodologically serious, and produced
a small number of sessions verified against ground truth (Price 1974, McMoneagle 1979).
**Contested:** whether those sessions demonstrate psi or lucky-plus-informed hits, and
whether the lab statistics establish anything. **Not established:** operational value —
both the proponents' own reviewer (Utts) and the skeptic (Hyman) agreed on that, and so
did the program's ultimate sponsor. The 1983 Gateway report (this archive's companion)
was one side-current of the same Cold War interest in consciousness tech, not a CRV manual.
